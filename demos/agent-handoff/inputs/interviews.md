# Agent-to-rep handoff: interview notes

*Fictitious research for a Queen Bee demo. Every person, company and number here is made up.*

Six 45-minute sessions with service reps and leads at customers of a service console that has an AI agent answering customers first and handing a conversation to a human rep when it can't finish. Sessions ran over two weeks. Each note is the researcher's summary, with the participant's words in quotes.

---

## P1. Adrienne Montero, Customer Support Specialist, Altitude Bankers (Finance)

- Gets 30 to 40 handoffs a day. Reads the agent's summary first, then scrolls the full transcript "every single time, because the summary has been wrong before."
- The summary once said a customer's card was "already frozen." It wasn't. She froze it late and the customer lost money. "One bad summary and you never trust it again."
- What she wants: "Tell me what you checked and what you didn't. I don't need you to sound sure."

## P2. Advik Kodhanda, Support Team Lead, University Hospital (Healthcare)

- The handoff opens in a new panel beside the patient's case. His reps "lose the case" because the panel covers the timeline.
- Reps re-ask patients for their date of birth after every handoff, because policy says identity must be re-checked by a human. Patients complain: "I just told your robot."
- "The policy isn't going away. But maybe the agent could do the check in a way we'd accept."

## P3. Akari Yoshikawa, Customer Support Specialist, McBride Media Group (Media)

- Likes the summary. Uses it as is on simple cases (billing, password resets). "On easy ones it's great. On weird ones it's useless."
- Wants to take over a conversation before the agent gives up. Today she can only watch. "I can see it going badly and I can't jump in."

## P4. Aleeza Clarke, Director of Customer Support, East Group (Technology)

- Owns the handoff rules. Set the agent to hand off after 3 failed answers. Thinks it's too late, "but if I set it to 1, my team drowns."
- Measures handle time after handoff. It went up 22% since the agent launched, because the conversations reps get are the hard ones, and they arrive with the customer already annoyed.
- "Nobody on my team decided the agent should try three times. I picked a number."

## P5. Adrian Fregoso, Customer Support Specialist, Bowman Mill Supplies (Manufacturing)

- Reads the transcript, never the summary. "The transcript is the truth."
- Says the hardest part is the customer's mood: "The summary says 'customer wants a refund.' The transcript shows they've asked four times and they're furious. That changes how I open."

## P6. Alicia Han, Support Operations Manager, Sansome Attorneys (Legal)

- Wants a record of what the agent told the customer before the rep took over, for compliance. Today it's only in the transcript, mixed in with everything else.
- "If the agent promised something, I need to know before my rep says the opposite."
